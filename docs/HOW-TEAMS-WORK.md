# Nicobar Tracker: How Teams Work Differently

Transcribed from the sponsor's draft, 8 Oct 2026. Example names replaced with placeholders because the repo is public. The original doc is in the team channel.

Nicobar has many teams. These four are a sample of the different ways they work. Every tracker shares one core: owner, task, due date, status, a one-line note and an update log. On top of that core, each type switches on what it needs.

## 1. Launch Project: one date, many teams

Example: [a new category launch].

A new product or category going live on a fixed date. The work is mostly internal and split by function: visual design, sourcing, approvals, finance and merch. One team's work often waits on another's. Each vertical coordinates with its own vendors and partners.

Needs: a launch countdown and phase, task dependencies, and a project owner approval step.

## 2. Run: ongoing, no end date

Example: [a three-person run team].

Day-to-day work across standing workstreams such as [five standing workstreams]. Work is grouped by project, so each project's tasks show in one view, but the setup still had to be customised. A team of three runs many threads at once, like a mini Nicobar, and each person owns parts of different projects.

Needs: each person's open and overdue items, a record of pushed dates, reminders, and recurring tasks.

## 3. Partner: two sides, one launch

Example: brand collabs ([a brand collab partner]), [an external agency] (e.g. [a quarterly plan]).

Nicobar and an external partner deliver together toward a launch or project delivery date. Every deliverable has a Nicobar owner and a partner owner, and each is responsible for their own side. For example, on a collab with [a brand collab partner], both [a brand collab partner] and Nicobar do part of the design work.

Open question: do we track the partner's tasks as well, or only Nicobar's?

Needs: an owner on each side, dated checkpoints, a list of what the partner needs from us, and tasks drafted from meeting notes.

## 4. Pipeline: styles moving through stages

Example: Design.

For each season or drop, SKUs and styles move through moodboard, sketch, sample, fit, approval, handoff. Sourcing runs alongside.

Needs: a stage board showing how many styles are at each stage, a season or drop calendar, design approvals, and sourcing dependencies.

Assumption: the Design team may not run whole projects end to end.

## At a glance

| | Launch | Run | Partner | Pipeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unit of work | Task | Task | Deliverable | Style or SKU |
| Grouped by | Function | Workstream | Deliverable | Stage |
| Time anchor | Launch date | None | Launch date + checkpoints | Season or drop |
| Main view | What blocks launch | My open + overdue | Next two weeks | Styles per stage |

## What this means for slice one

- The shared core is exactly the first build slice (see `docs/RESEARCH-PLAN.md`).
- The four types are switches on top of that core, not four tools.
- The Run type is the pilot shape.
- Launch adds dependencies and a countdown.
- Partner adds a second owner and the open question above.
- Pipeline is a different unit of work and is out of scope for the pilot.
